## 3. Constitution: rules for the budget meeting

All CPR references are to s51561 (edition footer "4.1-15.07.26"); BPFR to s51528.

### 3.1 Notice of budget amendments

- **No specific notice deadline for budget amendments is printed** in the CPR or BPFR held.
- Motions on notice need written notice "not later than seven working days before the date of the meeting" (CPR rule 11, page 9).
- "to amend a motion" may be moved without notice (CPR rule 13(g), page 10). The chair may require a motion to be written down before it is discussed (rule 14.2, page 10).
- Practice in 2026: the Conservative amendment "had been published in a supplementary agenda earlier in the day" (FC-MIN-0326, page 5). The Labour amendment was also in a supplementary agenda (b12916).

### 3.2 S151 sign-off of amendments

- **No rule requiring the S151 Officer to sign off or certify an alternative budget or amendment is printed** in the CPR, BPFR or Articles held.
- Practice: the Conservative amendment states "The Director of Resources (Section 151 Officer) has confirmed that the following are possible and realistic" and "This amendment is fully aligned with the S151 Officer's confirmed possibilities and uses only levers already discussed and accepted as feasible" (FC-SUPP-CON, pages 3 and 4).
- Related rule, CPR rule 12 "Motions on Expenditure" (page 9): a motion not moved on an Executive recommendation which "if carried would materially increase the expenditure or reduce the revenue of any service which is managed by the Council, or would involve capital expenditure, shall when moved and seconded stand referred to the Executive without discussion and considered at a future meeting of the Council." How this applies to budget amendments is not stated; in March 2026 both amendments were debated (the Conservative one reduced council tax income but was moved and voted on).
- The S151 Officer must report on robustness and reserves under section 25 of the Local Government Act 2003 (section 7 below).

### 3.3 Order of debate and amendments

- Ordinary meetings consider "proposals from the executive in relation to the Council's budget and policy framework" (CPR rule 2(ix), page 3).
- No speeches until seconded (rule 14.1, page 10). A seconder may reserve their speech (rule 14.3).
- Amendments must be relevant and either refer the matter, leave out words, leave out and insert, or add words, "as long as the effect of (ii) to (iv) is not to negate the motion" (rule 14.6(a), page 11).
- "Only one amendment may be moved and discussed at any one time" (rule 14.6(b)). If an amendment falls, others may be moved (14.6(c)). If carried, the amended motion becomes the substantive motion (14.6(d)) and the chair reads it out (14.6(e)).
- Alteration of motion: a member may alter a motion of which notice was given "with the consent of the meeting", signified without discussion; only alterations which could be made as an amendment (rule 14.7, page 12). This was used on 3 March 2026 to fold the Labour amendment into the Administration's motion.
- Withdrawal needs the consent of the meeting and the seconder (rule 14.8).

### 3.4 Speeches and right of reply

- "No speech may exceed five minutes without the consent of the chair" (rule 14.4, page 11). Speeches must be on the question, a personal explanation or a point of order.
- A member who has spoken may not speak again except: once on another member's amendment; to move a further amendment if the motion has been amended; on the main issue after first speaking on an amendment; in reply; on a point of order; by personal explanation; or to read for an incapacitated member (rule 14.5(a) to (g), page 11).
- Right of reply (rule 14.9, page 12): the mover of a motion replies at the end of the debate. On an amendment, "The mover of the amendment shall exercise their right to reply to the debate on the amendment immediately before the mover of the motion exercises their right of reply to the debate on the amendment. Immediately after the reply by the mover of the original motion, the amendment shall be put to the vote." A reply may not introduce new matter (14.9(f)); after a reply the decision is taken without further discussion (14.9(g)).
- Members stand to speak and address the meeting through the Mayor (rule 20.1, page 17). When the Mayor stands, any member speaking must sit and the meeting must be silent (rule 20.2).

### 3.5 Points of order and personal explanation

- Point of order (rule 14.12, pages 13 to 14): "A Member may raise a point of order at any time. The chair will hear them immediately. A point of order may only relate to an alleged breach of these Council Procedure Rules or the law. The Member must indicate the Rule or law and the way in which he/she considers it has been broken. The ruling of the chair on the matter will be final."
- Personal explanation (rule 14.13, page 14): "may only relate to some material part of an earlier speech by the member which may appear to have been misunderstood in the present debate. The ruling of the chair on the admissibility of a personal explanation will be final."

### 3.6 Closure motions

Rule 14.11 (page 13). At the end of another member's speech, a member may move without comment: to proceed to next business; that the question be now put; to adjourn the debate; to adjourn the meeting.
- Next business: if seconded and the chair thinks the item sufficiently discussed, the mover of the original motion replies, then the procedural motion is voted on.
- Question be now put: if seconded and the chair thinks it sufficiently discussed, the procedural motion is voted on; if passed, the mover of the original motion replies, then the motion is put.
- Adjourn: if the chair thinks the item has not been and cannot reasonably be sufficiently discussed, the procedural motion is voted on without a right of reply.
- "a ruling by the Chair that a matter has been sufficiently debated shall be final" (14.11(e)).

### 3.7 Voting, recorded votes and the casting vote

- Simple majority "of those Members voting and present in the room at the time the question was put" (rule 16.1, page 14).
- Casting vote (rule 16.2, page 14): "If there are equal numbers of votes for and against, the chair will have a second or casting vote. There will be no restriction on how the chair chooses to exercise a second or casting vote." The Mayor also votes as an ordinary member first (the Mayor's name appears in both recorded votes on 3 March 2026, FC-MIN-0326 pages 6 and 8).
- Recorded vote on demand of one quarter of members, by standing (rule 16.4, page 15); any member may have their own vote recorded (16.5).
- Recorded votes at budget meetings (rule 16.7, pages 15 to 16), mirroring the 2014 statutory regulations: "Immediately after any vote is taken on a budget decision at the budget decision meeting of the Council, there must be recorded in the minutes of the proceedings of that meeting the names of the persons who cast a vote for the decision or against the decision or who abstained from voting. This will include a recorded vote on any amendment proposed at the meeting." The "budget decision meeting" is one that makes a calculation under sections 31A, 31B, 34 to 36A, 42A, 42B, 45 to 49, 52ZF or 52ZJ of the Local Government Finance Act 1992, or issues a precept. The rule text does not name the 2014 regulations.
- Rules 1 to 8, 12 and 16 to 23 cannot be suspended (rule 22.1, page 18).

### 3.8 Quorum and duration

- Quorum of Council: one quarter (rule 7, page 4).
- A meeting that has lasted three hours adjourns unless a majority vote to continue (rule 8.1, page 5); a motion to continue beyond three hours may be moved without notice (13(l)).
- Public question time: first 30 minutes; 5 minutes per speaker; notice by 5.00 pm the day before (rule 9.1, page 5).

### 3.9 Remote attendance

**NOT FOUND.** No held constitution document provides for remote attendance or remote voting at Full Council. CPR rule 20.1 requires members to stand to speak.

### 3.10 Budget and Policy Framework Procedure Rules (s51528)

- Executive publishes a timetable and consults; "The consultation period shall in each instance be not less than four weeks" (rule 2(a), page 1). The scrutiny committee may respond (2(b)).
- Once the Executive approves firm proposals, the Head of Legal and Democratic Services refers them to Council (2(c)).
- Council's options (rule 2(d), page 1): "the Council may adopt the executive's proposals, amend them, refer them back to the executive for further consideration, or in principle, substitute its own proposals in their place."
- Effect (rule 2(e)): "If it accepts the recommendation of the executive without amendment, the Council may make a decision which has immediate effect. Otherwise, it may only make an in-principle decision. In either case, the decision will be made on the basis of a simple majority of votes cast at the meeting."
- The "deemed" process (rule 2(g), page 2): "An in-principle decision will automatically become effective 5 working days from the date the Council's decision is published, unless the leader informs the Head of Legal and Democratic Services in writing within that period that he/she objects to the decision becoming effective and provides reasons why."
- Leader's objection (rule 2(h)): Council meets "within a further 10 working days" to reconsider, and may approve the Executive's recommendation or a different decision, each by simple majority. The decision is then implemented immediately (2(i)).
- Council also sets the extent of virement (2(j)); the Mayor resolves any conflict in the process (2(k), referring to Article 5.01(7)).
- Virement: service managers may vire across budget heads "where each individual virement does not exceed £20,000" (rule 5(b), page 4).
- Decisions outside the budget go to Council (rule 3); urgent ones need the scrutiny chair's agreement (rule 4).
- Note: the BPFR do not mention the statutory 11 March deadline or any separate rule for the council tax resolution. Whether the Leader's objection procedure is in practice used for the budget is not stated in any held document.
- Precedent of referral back: at Full Council on 4 February 2026 the Council asked the Executive to reconsider fee rises; the Executive minute reads "In response to the recommendation from 4th February 2026 Full Council, requesting that the Executive should reconsider the proposed increases to bereavement and commercial waste charges", and the Executive then froze bereavement charges "at a cost of £35k, to be funded from the Revenue Support Reserve" (FC-SUPP-CT, Executive minute 93, page 13 and recommendation ix, page 14). The 4 February 2026 minutes themselves are not held.

### 3.11 What the budget is (Article 4)

"The budget includes the allocation of financial resources to different services and projects, proposed contingency funds, the council tax base, setting the council tax and decisions relating to the control of the Council's borrowing requirement, the control of its capital expenditure and the setting of virement limits." (ART, Article 4.01(b), page 6.) Approving the budget is reserved to Council (Article 4.02(b), page 6).
