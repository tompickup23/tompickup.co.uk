Castleford care home in Clitheroe had some unusual visitors: a bearded dragon, a skunk and a skinny pig. They were brought in for an animal safari, organised because animal therapy can stir memories, lower anxiety and get people talking to each other.

Castleford is one of the care homes that Lancashire County Council runs itself. I want to tell this story because of who paid for the day.

## Glenda

Glenda Pickover lived at Castleford. She died in January 2026, aged 81. Her family have raised money for the home since, including a sponsored walk across Morecambe Bay that some of the staff joined. The animal safari was funded by a donation in her memory.

Her daughter, Beverley Howard, remembered birds of prey flying round the living room on a day she visited with her three-year-old, and a VE Day street party where her mother, who barely spoke by then, sang along to Que Sera Sera. Glenda loved animals, so an animal safari in her name felt right to the family. "She would have enjoyed it and I'm sure it would make her smile."

## The residents

Joyce Gibson had not been around animals for a long time and found them delightful. Margot Fall, who grew up on a farm with cows, sheep and horses, offered the best line of the day: "They're better than people." And then, more seriously, that some of the others never see animals, so it was good for them.

What I said:

> Experiences like this make a real difference to residents and their wellbeing, and the animal safari also created memories and sparked conversations among the people who live at Castleford.
>
> We're incredibly grateful to Glenda's family for their generosity which allowed staff to create such a special day in her memory.

## Why it belongs on this site

Most of what I publish about adult social care is about money, models and buildings, because that is where the decisions are. But the point of all of it is an afternoon like this one: a room full of residents laughing at a skunk. Glenda's daughter put it better than I can: "The staff are so caring and lovely towards the residents and there is always a lot going on." That is what a council care home is for.
