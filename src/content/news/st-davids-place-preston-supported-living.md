A new supported living scheme has opened in Preston. St Davids Place has 14 modern, accessible apartments with communal spaces and a garden, and it provides long-term affordable homes for people with a learning disability and for autistic people. Building finished in February 2026 and tenants have now moved in.

Adult social care is my brief at County Hall, so let me set out what has opened and what it tells you about the direction we are going in.

## What has opened

Fourteen self-contained apartments. Shared spaces and a garden. Staff available 24 hours a day. The scheme was developed by RWP, one of the largest providers of supported living accommodation in the country, in partnership with Lancashire County Council. Support for the tenants comes from Affinity Trust.

RWP is part of Progress Housing Group and supports more than 4,500 tenancies for people with a learning disability, autistic people and people with a mental health condition. The group as a whole owns and manages around 12,000 homes, mostly in the North West. The other partners named on the delivery were Thornber and Walker, Studio John Bridge Architects, Beech Jackson Partnerships, Whitefield and Brown, and Aegis and Reid Jones.

What I said at the opening:

> It's fantastic that people have now moved in here and call this development their home.
>
> We work closely with our housing and care partners to provide high-quality homes that support people to live independently, be part of the local community and with the reassurance of staff being available 24/7.

Nicola Byrne, RWP's director of growth and partnerships, said the scheme offers homes where people can live with greater independence, dignity and choice, and that it strengthens the wider community too.

## The direction of travel

Under the county council's Housing with Care and Support Strategy, the council is modernising its supported living offer and building more apartments and bungalows, so that people can have their own front door. St Davids Place is what that looks like built.

Fourteen is fourteen. It does not clear the need in Preston, let alone the county. But every one of those tenancies is an adult who now has a key, a kitchen and a door that is theirs. The honest test of a strategy is not the first scheme. It is the second, the fifth and the twelfth, and how fast they follow.
