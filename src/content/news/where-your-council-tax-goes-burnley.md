## Most Burnley homes are Band A

Every home sits in a band from A to H. Councils quote Band D as the standard, and every other band is a fixed fraction of it set in law: Band A pays two thirds of Band D, Band H pays double.

In Burnley, Band D is unusual. Six in ten homes are Band A; about one in fourteen is Band D.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup> So most bills here look like the Band A row.

## Who gets it

Burnley Council sends every bill and collects all of it, then passes most of it on. Of every pound you pay, the split is the same in every band:

In 2025/26, the latest year published, Burnley had £60.7 million due and collected £57.1 million of it by 31 March 2026: 94.09%. The England rate was 95.6%. Money not in by March keeps being collected afterwards.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup>

## Council tax is only part of each budget

Each body also gets business rates and government grants. Council tax pays for:

- **53.9%** of the county council's £1,329.3 million net budget<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>
- **38.4%** of Burnley Council's £22.4 million net budget<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>
- **31.7%** of Lancashire Police's £442.6 million budget, with the police grant paying most of the rest<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-6">6</a></sup>
- **56.0%** of Lancashire Fire's £80.5 million net budget<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-7">7</a></sup>

## Where the county council share goes

A Band A home pays the county council £1,201.17 a year. Shared out the way the county's net budget is:<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>

| Area | From a Band A bill | From a Band D bill |
|---|---:|---:|
| Adults, health and wellbeing | £504.66 | £756.99 |
| Education and children's services | £240.11 | £360.17 |
| Roads, transport, waste and environment | £236.65 | £354.98 |
| Corporate budgets, including debt costs | £135.63 | £203.44 |
| IT, finance, legal, HR and the chief executive | £84.12 | £126.17 |

## Adult social care

Adult social care is my portfolio as Cabinet Member, and it is the county council's largest single cost. It takes **£512.85** of a Band A bill, about £9.86 a week (£769.27 at Band D).<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>

Of that, **£206.22** at Band A (£309.33 at Band D) is the adult social care precept, which has built up since 2016/17 and must be spent on care.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-8">8</a></sup>

At the end of the first quarter of 2026/27, 17,245 people were in long-term care.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-9">9</a></sup> The three largest groups:

| Group | From a Band A bill | People supported | Average cost a week each |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Older people and people with physical disabilities | £231.55 | 12,150 | £625 |
| People with learning disabilities and autism | £181.82 | 3,824 | £1,038 |
| People with mental health needs | £41.21 | 1,645 | £751 |

The county council puts it this way:

> "Around 55% of the council's budget is spent on approximately 5% of Lancashire's population."<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-10">10</a></sup>

## The challenge for next year

The consultation sets out the scale of it: "Even if council tax increased by the maximum amount currently permitted (4.99%), the council would still need to identify around £79 million of savings and efficiencies to balance next year's budget."<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-10">10</a></sup>
