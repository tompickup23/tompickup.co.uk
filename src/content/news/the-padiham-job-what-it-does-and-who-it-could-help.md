[The Padiham Job](/the-padiham-job/) is a free game that runs in your browser, on a phone or a computer. You walk the real streets of Padiham town centre, past the real Town Hall, the real shops and the real river. It asks for no name and no account, it has no adverts and no chat, and your progress is kept on your own device.

It now has a use beyond fun, and that comes first here. The rest of the game follows.

One thing first. I am the county council's cabinet member for adult social care. This game is my own project on my own website. It is not a council service, and the council has not adopted or endorsed it.

And a second. The game is a prototype example, shown to illustrate an idea. It is not finished and not ready for release. It may contain mistakes. It is offered as it stands, and no liability is accepted for any use made of it or any reliance placed on it.

## Practice routes: getting to know a walk before you do it

For some people a new walk is hard. A young person with special educational needs learning to get to college alone. An adult with a learning disability starting at a new day service. Someone anxious who has not been into town for a long time. For them, not knowing what is round the next corner can be the whole problem.

A parent, teacher or travel trainer can now set a route in the game:

1. Open the game and walk the route yourself.
2. At the start, and at each turning point, open the menu and choose "Add where I am standing".
3. Copy the link and send it to the person who will walk it.

When they open the link, the game changes. They start at the first spot. They are shown one marker at a time: "Walk to the marker by Boyes." The markers come in the same order every time. There is no clock, no score, no pubs, no litter to chase and fewer people on the street. A button takes them back to the start to walk it again, and the game counts how many times they have.

A route can include a bus ride: add a spot at the stop where they get on, and another near the stop where they get off.

The link holds the spots on the map and nothing else. It carries no name and no words, and nothing about the person walking it is stored or sent.

### Or pick a trip from a list

There is now a quicker way, which does not need you to walk the route in the game first. Choose "Practise a trip" on the start screen or in the menu. Pick where the trip starts and where it ends from the named places and bus stops. The game offers the walk, and a bus as well when it would save a good part of the walking. The person then sees the whole trip as a short list of steps before they set off, and one step at a time once they do.

The link for a trip holds the two places and the bus as short codes. It sits after the # in the address, a part that browsers do not send to the website.

### The way follows the streets

The arrow used to point straight at the next place, through buildings and across the river. It now follows the streets and footpaths, as a person would. The yellow line on the small map shows the same way, and "Where next?" gives it in words, with the next turn: "In 55 metres, turn left into Inskip Street."

There is a full map too. Press M, or press the small map. It shows the town centre with its place names, the bus stops you can ride from in the game, where you are, where you are going and the way between the two. It can be dragged and zoomed, and a line of text above it gives the same information in words.

### What it is not

It does not teach road safety. There is no traffic in the game, so nobody should learn to cross a road from it, and the start screen says so. The way it shows takes no account of steps, dropped kerbs or the safest place to cross, so it is not a step-free route and may not be the way a trainer would choose. It covers the town centre only. And I have not yet tried it with learners, so I cannot tell you that it works.

It is meant for one job: making a place familiar before the real walk, alongside the person who is teaching it. It does not replace that person.

## The rest of the game

The start screen now offers three ways to play. **Explore** is a wander with no job and no clock. **Jobs** is deliveries around town, one after another. **Get somewhere** is the practice trip described above. You can change between Explore and Jobs from the menu at any time.

- **Take jobs around town.** Collect a tray of pies from the bakery and get them to the pub. Carry bunting to the Town Hall. Each job sends you between real places. There is no clock unless you switch it on.
- **Do a good turn.** Once the first jobs are done, one delivery in three is for a neighbour: a hot dinner, a get well card, the morning paper. It pays nothing but thanks.
- **Pick up litter.** It lies on the pavements. Walk over it and it is gone.
- **Say hello.** The nearest passer-by stops and waves back.
- **Find the history.** Twenty blue plaques stand around the town, each with one fact and its source. The game keeps a journal of the ones you have found.
- **Try today's job.** One job a day, the same three stops for everyone, so you can compare with a friend.
- **Collect stamps.** Thirteen of them, for things like ten jobs done, fifty pieces of litter or a mile covered.
- **Send a postcard.** A picture of wherever you are standing, with the place name under it.
- **Catch a bus.** The stops in the town centre work. Pick your bus by its number and where it is going, wait for it, and ring the bell before your stop.
- **Get about your way.** On foot, in a wheelchair or in a powerchair. It changes how you look and nothing else, and some of the people you pass use wheelchairs too.

You can walk, run or sprint, and jump for the fun of it. The controls are shown one line at a time as you play, and you can change how you look or start a new game from the menu. The play area is 1,100 metres by 760 metres of the town centre, with 68 named places in it.

## Who else it could help

**Schools and families.** Every plaque gives its source, so a teacher can check each fact. A class could find all twenty and then walk the real street.

**People who find games hard to use.** The clock is off unless you ask for it. The settings include a calm town, larger text, stronger contrast, less movement on screen and messages that stay until you close them. The whole game can be played from a keyboard, and the way to the next place is given in words as well as by an arrow and a map. On a phone, the thumbstick comes to wherever your thumb lands.

**Anyone who cannot have the pubs on screen.** [The plain version](/the-padiham-job/?edition=clean) keeps the pub doors shut and leaves out the caricature by the Town Hall. Practice routes always use it.

**Anyone who loves the town.** Picking up litter, doing a good turn and saying hello are small things. They are also what a good town is made of, and the game rewards them.

## What it cannot do yet

- It has no moving traffic and no working crossings. The buses are the only vehicles that move.
- The bus numbers and stops come from OpenStreetMap and may be out of date. The game shows no times.
- The wheelchair and the powerchair were drawn without advice from a wheelchair user. If something is wrong, tell me.
- It stops at the edge of the town centre.
- The way it shows is the shortest by the streets. It takes no account of steps, dropped kerbs or the safest place to cross.
- A few building outlines sit on a road in the map data. The way goes round them, which can make it longer than the real walk.
- A practice route cannot show a photograph of the real corner, which is where I think it would help most.
- It is one person's project and it is still being built.

## Try it and tell me

If you teach, support or care for someone who might use a practice route, please [set one up](/the-padiham-job/?edition=clean#practice) and [tell me how it went](/contact/), good or bad. Three things help most: which route you set, whether the person used it before the real walk, and where they got stuck.
