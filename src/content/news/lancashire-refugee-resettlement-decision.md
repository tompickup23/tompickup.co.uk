On Thursday 3 September I sat in Committee Room B at County Hall as one of ten members of the Lancashire County Council cabinet. Item 10 on the agenda was refugee resettlement. We agreed that the county council should stop being the coordinating authority for the Home Office resettlement schemes, and notify the Home Office of that decision and our reasons.

The reporting since has been loud in both directions. So this is my attempt to set out what we actually decided, using only the published report and the minutes. Nothing here comes from the confidential appendix, which was considered in private and stays there.

Every figure in this article comes from the officer report to Cabinet of 3 September 2026 and the published minutes of that meeting, both available on the county council's website. Where a claim is contested, I have said so.

## What the schemes are

Lancashire County Council is the coordinating authority, on behalf of all 15 Lancashire councils, for five Home Office schemes: the Afghan Relocations and Assistance Policy, the Afghan Citizens Resettlement Scheme, the Afghanistan Resettlement Route, the UK Resettlement Scheme, and the Afghan Resettlement Programme.

The first thing to be clear about, because it is the most common confusion, is who these people are. Everyone on these schemes arrived in this country legally and holds indefinite leave to remain. Four of the five schemes exist for Afghans who worked with or alongside British forces, or who were put at risk by the February 2022 government data incident. This is not asylum accommodation. It is not hotels. Asylum seekers are a Home Office responsibility handled through its own contractors, and none of that was in front of us.

The second thing is that these schemes are already ending. The Afghanistan Resettlement Route was discontinued in July 2025. Two of the Afghan schemes closed to new applicants in March 2025, and Lancashire's remaining responsibilities under them run out in February 2028. The Afghan Resettlement Programme is winding down, with all relocations finished by December 2028. Lancashire's councils have not committed to take further UK Resettlement Scheme families after December 2026.

That last pairing matters, and I want to deal with it honestly rather than let someone else do it for me.

Around 350 properties in Lancashire have been used by people on these schemes since 2016, of which about 180 were still in use at the end of March. The vast majority are private rentals, found by the families themselves or by the Home Office. Set against 41,954 households on housing waiting lists across Lancashire, this decision is not a housing policy and I am not going to pretend it is one. Anyone telling you that ending the county council's coordinating role will free up housing is misleading you. Housing allocation is a district and borough function. It was never ours to give.

## The money, straight

This decision does not save the county council money, and I have not seen a version of the argument where it does.

The £18m is Home Office grant, not council tax. The £6.530m sitting in reserve is ringfenced and cannot be spent on potholes or care homes. Our critics have made this point repeatedly, and on the narrow question of whether withdrawal produces a saving they are right.

The report is candid about the other side of it. Some of that grant currently pays for statutory services the county council has to provide anyway: early help, access to education, interpretation. Under the option we agreed, the ability to charge those costs to the grant goes. Officers put a high level estimate on that at around £0.800m a year for the existing cohort, a little over £1,000 per person. The report says a managed exit which secures continued funding from whoever takes over "would minimise financial impact on the council". That is the outcome we now have to negotiate for, and I would rather say that plainly than claim a saving that does not exist.

## Why I supported it anyway

If it saves nothing, why do it. Three reasons, and none of them is about the people on the schemes.

**There is no duty here.** The report puts it flatly: "There is no statutory duty upon the county council to be the coordinating or accountable body for these schemes." Not every council does this. Two tier areas across the country run a mix of arrangements. Lancashire took this on in 2016 by agreement with the other 14 councils and has renewed it annually ever since, largely out of habit.

**Reorganisation makes the current setup obsolete.** Local Government Reorganisation abolishes the county council. Housing and temporary accommodation duties move from the district councils to the new unitary authorities. Running national resettlement administration through an authority that will not exist, and which holds none of the housing duties the schemes depend on, is the wrong structure for whatever comes next. Better to hand it over deliberately now than to have it fall over on vesting day.

**The schemes are closing anyway.** Given the dates above, the honest question was never whether Lancashire administers this forever. It was whether the county council winds down its role in an orderly way, or carries it to the end of an organisation that is itself being wound up.

## What we had to weigh against it

I do not think it helps anyone to pretend this was a free decision.

The report placed Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights in front of us, on the ground that any change affects family life and integration for the households concerned, and told us that any interference must be proportionate and justified. It placed the public sector equality duty in front of us, with an Equality Impact Assessment identifying the groups most likely to be affected: refugee households, and county council staff employed to work with them, across race, nationality, religion, age and disability. It warned that losing the coordinating role means losing direct data on the refugee population in Lancashire, which makes planning services harder.

Those are real costs and they sit on our side of the ledger. The mitigation, in every case, is a managed transition rather than a cliff edge, which is why the decision runs to no later than 31 March 2027 and why the Home Office has asked for a phased handover.

## What this decision does not do

It does not stop refugees settling in Lancashire. The report is explicit: withdrawal "would not automatically result in an end to refugees resettling in Lancashire". Families can still be placed here by other authorities, and can move here of their own accord.

It does not remove a single duty the county council owes to anyone living here. Refugees who live in Lancashire are Lancashire residents, and they get the same statutory services as everyone else, from the same council, on the same terms. That does not change.

## The argument I actually want to have

The thing this exposes is not local. It is that central government designs schemes like these, funds them on annual instructions it can change, and relies on councils to pick up the administrative burden and the statutory costs that outlast the grant.

Lancashire carried that for the best part of a decade. The Home Office has now been told we are not carrying it by default any longer, and that if it wants continuity it needs to arrange it and fund it properly. That is the conversation I want, and it is with Whitehall, not with 745 people who came here legally and mostly because they helped us.

## What happens next

All of the opposition groups at County Hall have called the decision in. A special meeting of the Community, Cultural and Corporate Services Scrutiny Committee sits at 3pm on 11 September, and the decision cannot be implemented until scrutiny has dealt with it.

That is the process working as it should. Scrutiny's job is to test whether we reached this properly, and if the committee has found something we got wrong I would rather hear it there than read it later. I will not pretend to be neutral about a decision I took part in. But a call-in is not an inconvenience to be seen off, and if it comes back to cabinet it comes back to be reconsidered, not rubber stamped.
