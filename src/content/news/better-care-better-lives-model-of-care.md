Tomorrow, Thursday 1 October, Lancashire County Council's Cabinet will be asked to endorse Better Care, Better Lives. It is the county's future Model of Care for adult social care, and it is the most consequential document my portfolio has produced since I took it on.

So let me be precise about what it is, how it was made, and what it does not do.

## How it was built

In May 2026 Cabinet agreed the direction: a model of care built with the people who use it, not handed down to them, and no immediate changes to existing services while that work happened.

What followed is the part I am proudest of. The model was shaped by people who use care and support, by carers and families, by staff, by providers and by community organisations, working together. The council calls that co-production. What people said, over and over, was that trusted relationships, familiar faces, real choice and staying connected to their own area are what make the difference. The model starts from the person, not from the service.

## What it would change

For an individual or a family, it could mean three things. Help is easier to find. Conversations are joined up, because nobody should have to tell their story over and over again to get help. And the support you get is built around your strengths, your relationships and what you are trying to do, not around a menu.

Behind that sits more emphasis on early help and support in your own neighbourhood, and more choice for people who need formal care: flexible support at home, specialist housing, and high-quality 24-hour care, with specialist services closer to home wherever that can be done. Community and voluntary organisations get a bigger role in providing support. The council will work with health partners, providers and community organisations on dementia care, on regaining independence after illness or a hospital stay, and on support for people coming home.

What I said when the report was published:

> Good care is about much more than services. It is about helping people hold on to their independence, relationships, routines and sense of belonging.
>
> People have told us they want to be listened to, to have real choice and to get help before things reach crisis point. Nobody should have to tell their story over and over again to get help. This model is built around what they told us.
>
> This is the start of the detailed design, not the end of the conversation.
>
> Nothing in this report closes any service or building. The people who shaped this model will keep shaping it, alongside the staff and partners whose care people already value.

## What it does not do

I want to be blunt about this because the word "model" makes people nervous, and with good reason. The report to Cabinet does not propose, and does not seek approval for, the closure of any existing service or building. None. If any such proposal ever comes, it comes separately, with its own business case, its own assessments, its own consultation and its own decision. That is written into the report, and I will be held to it.

A model of care is a direction of travel. The test is whether, in a year, someone in Padiham who needs help finds it faster and has to tell their story fewer times than they do today. That is the measure I would like to be judged on.
