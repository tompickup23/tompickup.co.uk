St Martin's Court in Lancaster opens to its first tenants this summer. It is an Extra Care scheme: 92 affordable one and two-bedroom apartments for rent, for people aged 55 and over, with care and support on site around the clock for those who need it.

Adult social care is my brief at County Hall, so here is what has been built, by whom, and why this kind of housing matters more than its size suggests.

## What Extra Care is

It sits between an ordinary flat and a care home. You have your own apartment and your own tenancy. Care is there in the building, 24 hours a day, but it is tailored to you and you only draw on it as you need it. Anyone with a care need is assessed by Lancashire County Council and by Willowbrook, which the council has commissioned to provide the on-site care, so that the level of support fits the person now and can change as they do.

The point is to let people stay independent for longer, in a home they can grow old in, rather than moving into residential care because their flat no longer works for them.

## Who built it and where

The scheme was developed by Progress Housing Group with Lancaster City Council and the county council, on former University of Cumbria land, and built by Caddick Construction. Homes England grant funding helped pay for it. Two former military barracks buildings on the site, later used by the university, have been converted into further one and two-bedroom affordable apartments rather than demolished. Allocations will be run jointly by Progress, the city council and the county council.

What I said when the opening was announced:

> This development will give people the chance to live independently in high-quality, affordable homes, with care and support available when they need it.
>
> Working with our partners, we're proud to have helped to create a place where people can feel safe, supported and part of their local community.

## Why the number matters

Ninety-two is a lot of front doors. For a tenant who needs care, each one is a chance to stay in a home of their own instead of moving into residential care. For a county with an ageing population, that is the case for Extra Care. Whether it also saves public money is a figure I will publish when I have verified it at source.

The question I will keep asking is where the next one is. Lancaster has this. Padiham and Burnley West, which I represent, should be in the queue.
