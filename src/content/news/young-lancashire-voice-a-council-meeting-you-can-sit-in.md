[Young Lancashire Voice](/young-lancashire-voice/) is a prototype I have built for young people. You take a seat in the county council chamber, sit through a short meeting, move a motion of your own and see it put to the vote. It runs in a browser, on a phone or a computer. It asks for no name and no account, and nothing you do in it is collected, stored or sent.

The idea of a virtual council meeting was not mine. It came from Wayne Walsh, who lives in Burnley, and I am grateful to him for it. The design, the wording and any mistakes are mine.

One thing first. I am the county council's cabinet member for adult social care. This is my own project on my own website. It is not a council service, the council has not adopted or endorsed it, and it is not part of the UK Youth Parliament. It names no councillor and no party.

## What you do in it

1. **Write a motion.** A motion asks the council to do something. You build yours from set parts: something the county council runs, such as libraries, buses or the roads near schools; why it matters; what should happen; and how it is paid for. If you want to change something the county council does not run, the page tells you who does.
2. **Take your seat.** You choose a numbered seat in the chamber and the meeting begins. It follows the order of a real Full Council meeting: apologies, declarations of interest, public question time, councillors' questions, a report to agree, and then your motion.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> The Chairman calls you. You ask a question, move your motion, decide whether to accept a change to it, and have the last word before the vote.
3. **Ask Lancashire.** The same motion is then put as a statement, the way it would be put to young people across the county. One link carries it to [Lancashire Voice](/lancashire-voice/), my prototype for asking every resident, so the two answers could be read side by side.
4. **See what happens next.** The page ends with the real ways a young person can be heard today: a public question at Full Council, a petition, the Lancashire Youth Council, the Great Lancashire Debate, and a letter to your county councillor.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup><sup class="cite"><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup>

Every line the Chairman speaks comes with the same thing said in plain words underneath.

## What is new

**A whole class can run the meeting.** Choose "As a class, on one screen" when you take your seat. Nobody needs their own device. The page gives you:

- role cards to print: the Chairman, the Chief Executive, a Cabinet Member, the mover and seconder of the motion, speakers for and against, and reporters who write down what was decided;
- a clock for each speech, where ten seconds stand for each minute a councillor would get in the real chamber;
- a real vote. The Chairman asks for those in favour and those against, an adult counts the hands and types the numbers in, and the result is whatever the room decided;
- a casting vote for the class Chairman when the hands are equal;
- questions to talk over at the end, starting with whether anyone changed their mind and why.

**Tap a word to see what it means.** Words such as motion, seconder, amendment and casting vote are underlined wherever they first appear. Tap one and its meaning shows beneath.

**It can read to you.** Turn on "Read aloud" and each speaker's words are spoken by the voice built into your own device. There is a quiet sound option as well. Both stay off until you ask for them.

**It shows how the vote was won.** If you play on your own, the end of the meeting now sets out what moved votes: how you opened, whether you accepted the change, how you answered the question about money. Those numbers are made up for the game. The lesson in them is real.

**The room pays attention.** The figures on the benches now turn to face whoever is speaking.

**You can print your motion.** The motion paper prints on its own page.

## What is real and what is made up

The order of the meeting, the offices and the rules shown are real, and the rules are taken from the county council's standing orders.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup> Every speech, question and answer is made up. So is every vote count, unless you play as a class and count your own hands.

The room is built from simple shapes and drawn patterns. There is no photograph in it, the figures are not real people, and the seat positions are not the council's real seating plan.

## What it cannot do yet

- You cannot type your own words. That is on purpose: with no text box, nothing a child writes can be collected. A full version would let you write your own motion, and the words would stay on your device.
- The class meeting is new and has not yet been tried in a real classroom.
- The room has not been measured. It is drawn from what the chamber looks like, not from a survey of it.
- The other motions on the Ask Lancashire screen are examples written for the prototype. No votes are gathered from anyone.
- No decision has been made about Young Lancashire Voice or Lancashire Voice. Both are my prototypes, shown so people can see how the two would join.

## Try it and tell me

If you are a young person, a parent, a teacher or a youth worker and you try it, please [tell me how it went](/contact/), good or bad. Three things help most: which words were still too hard, where the meeting dragged, and whether anyone changed their mind in the debate.
