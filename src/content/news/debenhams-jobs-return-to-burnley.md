*By Tom Pickup, Lancashire county councillor and Cabinet Member for Adult Social Care.*

Debenhams Group, the company formerly known as Boohoo, is bringing its distribution operations back to Burnley. The group will keep its distribution centre at Heasandford, and global logistics firm GXO will run the site. Warehouse and distribution work will move back from Sheffield in early 2027. Burnley Council expects the move to create more than 1,200 jobs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

It is a turnaround few expected. In August 2025 the company said it planned to close the Burnley site and move the work to Sheffield, putting 1,251 jobs at risk.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-2">2</a></sup> The site closed at the end of February 2026.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-3">3</a></sup> The group has since sold its Sheffield distribution centre to Primark for £90 million, and the work is now coming home.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-4">4</a></sup>

---

## How it came back

Last year, after the closure was announced, I emailed Dan Finley, the Debenhams Group chief executive. I proposed a joint meeting with the company. That meeting brought him together with:

- Afrasiab Anwar MBE, the leader of Burnley Council
- Azhar Ali, the leader of the opposition at Lancashire County Council
- Brian Moore, the county's Cabinet Member for Economic Development
- Lukman Patel, Burnley Council's chief executive
- officers from both councils

Councillors from different parties and officers from two councils sat on the same side of the table for Burnley. Working together for Burnley has ultimately led to these jobs returning, along with contributions from our MP. Councillor Anwar thanked "all politicians who put party politics aside and worked as one for the good of the borough", and I want to thank the officers at both councils who did much of the work.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-1">1</a></sup>

## What the jobs mean for the area

These jobs matter because a lot of people round here are looking for work. In May 2026, the latest month DWP has published, 4,608 people in Burnley were on Universal Credit and expected either to look for work or to prepare for it. In Pendle the figure was 4,495, and in Hyndburn 3,759: 12,862 across the three boroughs.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

Most of them are in the "searching for work" group: 3,309 in Burnley, 3,345 in Pendle and 2,775 in Hyndburn. The rest are people DWP expects to prepare for work, for example through interviews or training, before they look for it.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-5">5</a></sup>

On the other side of the ledger, the ONS counted 702 new online job adverts in Burnley in September 2026, 547 in Pendle and 598 in Hyndburn. That counts adverts posted in the month, not open vacancies, and one job can be advertised more than once.<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-6">6</a></sup>

More than 1,200 jobs at one site is a big step for a labour market of this size. It will not, on its own, find work for everyone who needs it, which is why the councils working together has to carry on after the ribbon is cut.

## If you want one of the jobs

GXO has started recruiting for the Burnley site, and its recruitment page is at [gxo.jobs/Burnley](https://gxo.jobs/Burnley).<sup class="cite"><a href="#source-7">7</a></sup> When I checked on 24 October it had no individual vacancies listed yet, so it is worth checking back.
