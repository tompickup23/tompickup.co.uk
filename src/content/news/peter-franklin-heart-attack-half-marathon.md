Peter Franklin is an enablement support worker in Lancashire County Council's adult social care service. On Sunday 23 August he runs the Fleetwood half marathon. Two days later is the first anniversary of the heart attack that nearly killed him.

I want to tell this story because it is a good one, and because it is a chance to say what the Enablement Team actually does.

## What happened

Peter, who is 53 and lives in Fleetwood, had just married his wife Kim. Twenty-four hours after they landed in the Caribbean for their honeymoon he had a massive heart attack. Because of where they were, it was four hours before he reached a hospital, and then an ambulance transfer of 300 kilometres to a second hospital before he could have stents fitted. In all, it was 36 hours before that operation.

He flew home to Manchester with a full medical escort and went straight to Blackpool Victoria Hospital. Three days later he had a second heart attack, less severe, dealt with at once. He was diagnosed with severe heart failure and told he would be on medication for life. In December 2025 he had a defibrillator implanted under the skin to shock his heart if it goes into a dangerous rhythm.

In his own words, "I honestly don't know how I survived."

## What he did next

He started exercising. Slowly, then with heavier weights, then harder and longer, then running. Short distances first, then 5k, then one day he kept going and 10k became normal. Then he entered the half marathon, two days before the anniversary.

What I said when the council told his story:

> Peter's determination to get fitter and improve his health is truly inspiring, but it's clear that he brings resilience and positivity to all aspects of his life.
>
> Peter works in our Enablement Team where he helps people with learning disabilities and autism gain the confidence to live as independently as possible by teaching them skills from cooking to travelling independently and managing their finances.

## Why enablement matters

Peter's job deserves a word of explanation. Enablement is the unglamorous end of adult social care. It is a worker sitting with someone while they learn to cook a meal, catch a bus on their own, or run a bank account, so that they need less care later, not more. It is the difference between a service that manages people and one that gets out of their way.

It is done by people like Peter, who does that job with a defibrillator under his skin. Good luck on Sunday.
