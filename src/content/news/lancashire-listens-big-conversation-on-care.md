On Wednesday 28 October people from across Lancashire came to Crossgate Church in Preston to tell the county council what they want from adult social care. The event was called Lancashire Listens: The Big Conversation on Care. It was free, it was informal, and it was built around conversation rather than a platform and a slide deck.

Adult social care is my brief at County Hall, so I was there. Here is what the day was, what it was for, and what happens to what people said.

## Who came

People who receive care or support. People who look after a family member or a friend without pay. People who work in health or social care. And residents who simply wanted a say in how care is organised in the county they live in.

Instead of a conference, visitors moved between local organisations, talked, and gave feedback on what is working and what is not. The topics included helping people stay independent, support for unpaid carers, community support, technology, and making it easier to find information and get through the door of a service. County Councillor Martyn Sutton joined those conversations too.

## Why it matters beyond Preston

Baroness Louise Casey chairs the Independent Commission on Adult Social Care. Lancashire Listens was part of the commission's national Big Conversation on Care, and the feedback from the day will shape Lancashire's response to it, on the bigger questions facing social care nationally, including what people want and need from the care system in the future.

That is the point I want to land. Lancashire will be putting a view to the commission, and I would far rather that view was built from what carers and care users actually said in that room than from what officers and councillors assume they would say.

What I said after the event:

> Thank you to everyone who came along and spoke to us about the kind of care and support they want to see in the future.
>
> It was really valuable to hear directly from people about what matters to them, what works well and what they think could be better.
>
> Those views and experiences will now help shape Lancashire's response to the national commission and ensure voices from across the county are heard.

## The honest bit

One day in one church in Preston is a start, not a consultation of the whole county. Plenty of people who depend on care could not get to Preston on a Wednesday, and plenty of unpaid carers could not leave the person they care for. The next step is to take the same conversation to the places people already are, and to publish what we heard so that people can check we listened.

I will put the themes from the day on this site when the council has written them up, with the document they come from.
